Materials Sector

The foundation of all industries – everything physical starts here

How They Make Money

Before a factory can make anything, before a building can be built, before a car can be assembled – raw materials must exist. The Materials sector extracts resources from the earth and processes them into the basic inputs every other industry depends on. Cement, steel, aluminium, specialty chemicals, fertilizers, packaging, paints – these are not end products. They are the beginning of every other product.

Revenue flows in distinct ways across the sector. Cement companies earn by selling cement per tonne to construction contractors, real estate developers, and infrastructure projects. Volume multiplied by realization (price per tonne) is the entire revenue model. Steel companies earn from selling flat products (for auto and appliances) and long products (for construction) – revenue tied directly to global commodity price cycles. Specialty chemical companies earn significantly higher margins than commodity producers because their molecules are technically complex, serve specific industries like pharma or agrochemicals, and have limited competition. Fertilizer companies in India operate under heavy government price controls on urea – selling to farmers at government-set prices and receiving subsidy reimbursement from the government for the cost difference. Branded building materials companies – paints, tiles, pipes – earn brand premium margins by selling to retail consumers and contractors who value quality and brand trust over price.

Key Players in India

Cement: UltraTech Cement (India's largest, Aditya Birla Group), Ambuja Cements and ACC (Adani Group), Shree Cement (most cost-efficient large producer), Dalmia Bharat, JK Cement

Steel: Tata Steel (integrated global, Jamshedpur and UK), JSW Steel (India's largest by capacity), SAIL or Steel Authority of India (government-owned), JSPL or Jindal Steel and Power, Hindalco Industries

Specialty Chemicals: Asian Paints (India's largest paints company), Pidilite Industries (Fevicol adhesives), SRF Limited (fluorochemicals), PI Industries (agrochemicals CDMO), Deepak Nitrite

Fertilizers: Chambal Fertilisers, Coromandel International, IFFCO (world's largest fertilizer cooperative), NFL or National Fertilizers

Building Materials: Kajaria Ceramics (India's largest tile company), Astral Ltd (pipes and adhesives), Supreme Industries (plastic pipes), Cera Sanitaryware

Aluminium and Metals: Hindalco Industries (Asia's largest aluminium producer, owns Novelis globally), NALCO or National Aluminium Company (government-owned), Hindustan Zinc (Vedanta, world's second-largest zinc-lead miner)

3 Terms Every Learner Must Know

Realization (Per Tonne): The average price a cement or steel company receives per tonne of product sold. Revenue equals volume multiplied by realization. When commodity prices fall globally – due to oversupply or weak demand – realization falls directly, compressing revenue and margins even if volumes are maintained. Realization is the number to watch in every cement and steel quarterly result.

EBITDA Per Tonne: Operating profit earned per tonne of cement or steel sold. The single most important profitability metric for commodity producers. It varies with coal and limestone costs, power costs, and selling prices. The most efficient cement companies (Shree Cement) consistently earn higher EBITDA per tonne than peers because of superior energy efficiency and logistics.

China Plus One: The global trend of multinational companies reducing their manufacturing and sourcing dependence on China and adding a second sourcing country – often India. This trend has been the most powerful structural driver of India's specialty chemicals sector since 2020. When a global pharma company shifts sourcing from a Chinese plant to an Indian plant like SRF or PI Industries, it creates multi-year supply contracts that dramatically improve revenue visibility and capacity utilisation.

What to Watch as a Learner

  • Government infrastructure and housing capex budget each year – cement and steel volumes are directly driven by construction activity; every road, metro, and housing project translates into demand
  • China steel and chemical export data – when Chinese factories dump excess production into global markets at low prices, Indian steel and commodity chemical companies face margin pressure immediately; watch anti-dumping duty announcements
  • Coal and coking coal prices globally – coal is 25-35% of cement production cost and coking coal is the primary input for blast furnace steelmaking; commodity price spikes directly compress margins
  • USFDA approvals and specialty chemical export order wins – for specialty chemical companies, new long-term customer contracts and regulatory approvals are the forward revenue indicators
  • Capacity utilisation rates – for cement and steel, utilisation below 75% means fixed costs are only partially absorbed; watch for industry-level overcapacity signals

Value Chain

The Materials value chain begins at the earth itself. For cement, limestone is quarried, fired in a kiln at 1,450 degrees Celsius to produce clinker, blended with fly ash or slag, ground into powder, packaged, and shipped to distributors and construction sites. Limestone accounts for 85% of raw material cost and energy is the second largest input – companies with captive power plants have a structural cost advantage.

For steel, iron ore and coking coal enter a blast furnace to produce pig iron, which is converted to crude steel and then rolled into flat or long products for automotive, construction, or infrastructure use. An alternative route uses electric arc furnaces with scrap steel – cheaper and cleaner, and India is expanding this capacity rapidly.

For specialty chemicals, basic molecules derived from crude oil or natural gas go through multiple reactor synthesis steps to produce the final specialty molecule. Each additional synthesis step increases complexity, reduces competition, and raises margin. This is why a specialty chemical with seven synthesis steps earns 25% EBITDA margins while a commodity chemical earns 10%.

For fertilizers, natural gas is the feedstock for urea production. The government-subsidized distribution network ensures fertilizers reach farmers across India's 140 million hectares of agricultural land.

For paints and branded building materials, titanium dioxide and other pigments are processed into finished paints sold through a vast dealer network. Asian Paints' distribution network of 75,000+ dealers is itself a competitive moat.

Revenue Model

Cement revenue is volume in tonnes multiplied by realization in rupees per tonne. During infrastructure booms, demand exceeds capacity and realization rises. During slowdowns, overcapacity leads to price wars and realization falls. Cement is a regional market – freight costs make it uneconomical to transport cement more than 300-400km – so pricing varies significantly by region and local supply-demand balance.

Steel revenue follows a similar volume-times-price model but is more globally influenced – steel prices track international benchmarks and Chinese export pricing directly. When Chinese mills export surplus at low prices, Indian steel companies must choose between matching those prices (hurting margins) or losing volume.

Specialty chemicals earn through long-term supply contracts with global pharma, agriculture, and electronics companies. Once a specialty chemical supplier is qualified and audited by a global client – a process taking 12-24 months – they often supply that customer exclusively for years, creating extremely sticky and visible revenue.

Paints and branded building materials earn through a dealer-retail network with premium pricing power. Asian Paints commands significantly higher prices than regional competitors because of brand trust, colour matching technology, and distribution network strength. The tinting machine installed at every Asian Paints dealer creates switching costs.

Cost Structure

For cement, energy is the largest variable cost – coal for the kiln and electricity for grinding account for 25-35% of production cost. Companies with captive power plants or coal mines have a durable cost advantage. Logistics cost – moving heavy cement to construction sites – is 15-20% of total cost, making proximity to markets a structural advantage.

For steel, raw materials dominate – iron ore and coking coal together represent 55-65% of production cost. Companies with captive iron ore mines (Tata Steel, JSPL) have significant cost advantages. Steel is a capital-intensive industry – a new integrated steel plant costs ₹50,000-₹1,00,000 crore and takes 5-7 years to build.

For specialty chemicals, raw material costs are 45-55% of revenue but the key cost driver is the complexity of the synthesis process – yield losses, solvent recovery, waste disposal, and regulatory compliance all add cost. Skilled chemists and chemical engineers are the talent cost that creates the intellectual moat.

For fertilizers, natural gas is 70-75% of urea production cost. Government price control on urea selling price means fertilizer companies are entirely dependent on the government paying subsidy reimbursements on time – delayed subsidy payments create working capital stress.

Key KPIs

Volume Growth (%): Year-on-year growth in tonnes of cement, steel, or chemical produced and sold. Volume growth above industry average means market share gain. Below-average volume growth signals competitive weakness or capacity constraints.

Realization (₹ per tonne): Average selling price per unit. Rising realization signals a healthy demand-supply balance or premiumisation toward value-added products. Falling realization signals oversupply, pricing pressure, or commodity cycle downturn.

EBITDA Per Tonne: Operating profit per unit sold. The most important single number for cement and steel companies. India's best cement companies consistently earn ₹1,000-₹1,500 per tonne EBITDA.

Capacity Utilisation (%): What percentage of total installed capacity is currently being used. Below 75% means fixed costs are under-absorbed and margins are thin. Above 85% creates pricing power as buyers cannot easily find alternative suppliers.

Net Debt to EBITDA: Materials companies carry significant debt due to capital-intensive plant investments. Below 2x is comfortable. Above 3-4x becomes risky in a commodity downturn when EBITDA can fall 30-50% rapidly.

Working Capital Days: How many days of revenue are tied up in raw material inventory, work-in-progress, and receivables. High working capital – especially in fertilizer companies waiting for government subsidy reimbursement – increases financing cost and financial risk.

Growth Drivers

India's infrastructure supercycle is the most powerful demand driver for cement and steel – government spending on roads, railways, metro systems, airports, and affordable housing has been at historic highs. Every kilometre of highway consumes thousands of tonnes of cement and steel. India's 300 million tonne steel capacity target by FY30 reflects the scale of this opportunity.

Housing demand – both urban and rural – is a multi-decade structural driver for cement, tiles, pipes, paints, and sanitary ware. India's housing shortage runs into tens of millions of units. As incomes rise and urbanization continues, housing construction will sustain materials demand for decades.

China Plus One is transforming India's specialty chemicals sector. Global pharma, agrochemical, and electronics companies are actively shifting sourcing from China to India to reduce supply chain concentration risk. Indian specialty chemical companies with regulatory approvals and technical capabilities are capturing multi-year supply contracts that were previously going to Chinese manufacturers.

EV adoption is creating new demand vectors for aluminium (EVs use 2-3x more aluminium than ICE vehicles), copper (EV wiring and charging infrastructure), and specialty chemicals (battery electrolytes, separator films). Hindalco and Hindustan Zinc are direct structural beneficiaries of India's EV transition.

Green building and sustainability trends are driving demand for blended cement (lower carbon), energy-efficient tiles, recyclable aluminium packaging, and eco-friendly paints. Companies that lead the sustainability transition will have regulatory and reputational advantages in export markets facing carbon tariffs.

Risks

Commodity Price Cyclicality – The Core Risk: Steel prices can fall 20-40% from peak to trough in a global cycle. Cement realizations can compress sharply when construction slows. When revenue falls while fixed factory costs remain, EBITDA can halve or worse in a single year. The 2015-2016 global commodity downturn caused significant losses across Indian steel companies and triggered debt crises at overleveraged producers.

Chinese Dumping: When global steel or chemical demand slows, Chinese factories dump excess production into global markets at prices below Indian production costs. Indian companies must either accept margin compression or lose volume. Anti-dumping duties provide partial protection but are subject to political and trade negotiation pressures. This happened sharply to Indian steel companies in FY25-26.

High Capital Intensity and Debt: Building a new cement plant costs ₹5,000-₹8,000 crore. An integrated steel plant costs ₹50,000+ crore. Companies that over-invest at the peak of a commodity cycle and carry high debt into a downturn face existential risk – as demonstrated by the collapse of Bhushan Steel, Essar Steel, and Lanco in India's insolvency resolution cases.

Energy Cost Volatility: Coal is 25-35% of cement production cost. Coking coal is 55-65% of steel production cost. When global coal prices spike – as they did in 2021-22 following the Russia-Ukraine conflict – materials companies face margin compression that cannot be quickly passed through to buyers.

Government Policy Risk for Fertilizers: The entire fertilizer sector operates under government price control on urea. Delayed subsidy payments from the government create working capital stress. Any change in subsidy policy directly affects fertilizer company economics and volumes.

Regulatory and Environmental Risk: Mining and materials companies face increasingly stringent environmental regulations – forest clearances for limestone quarries, water usage restrictions, carbon emission limits, and effluent discharge standards. Regulatory delays or non-compliance can shut mines or plants, disrupting production and creating financial liability.
